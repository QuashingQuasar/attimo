## High-polyphenol olive oil carries up to ten times the polyphenols of supermarket oil

Polyphenols are the compounds that make fresh olive oil bitter and peppery, and most of olive oil's researched health effects come from them. Labs measure them in milligrams per kilogram of oil (mg/kg).

Typical supermarket oil carries 100–250 mg/kg. The EU sets a level of 250 mg/kg for its olive oil health claim, which says the polyphenols help protect blood fats from oxidative stress. The oils on this list start at 400 mg/kg, and the strongest carry more than 1,000.

The difference comes from the olive variety and the harvest date. Some varieties, such as Coratina from Puglia and Kalamon from Greece, carry more polyphenols by nature. Olives picked early, while still green, give less oil and more polyphenols.

## The oils are ranked by oleocanthal and oleacein, the two compounds labs agree on

Ranking oils by their total polyphenol figure would be the natural thing to do, but it would be misleading.

The totals come from different lab methods that count different compound sets in different units. The same oil can test 20% to 48% higher on one method than on another: Olivea's oil reads 874 mg/kg by one method and 1,046 by another.

This list uses the measure the high-phenolic field itself prefers: **oleocanthal + oleacein**, the two most-researched compounds in olive oil, printed as the "D1 index" on every University of Athens certificate. Unlike total polyphenol counts, these two single named molecules hold up across labs: in peer-reviewed testing on the same oils, oleocanthal measured by two different methods agreed within ±10% (Molecules, 2021).

An oil can only be ranked if its producer publishes the compound values. Oils with a stated total and nothing else appear in a second, unranked list.

Each oil is ranked on its 2025/26 analysis, the latest harvest for which every oil has one. Where a 2026/27 analysis is already out, the entry gives it too. The full method is on the [Olive Oil Polyphenol Index](/polyphenol-index) page.

## Supermarkets rarely sell high-polyphenol olive oil

None of the supermarket brands we checked prints a polyphenol figure: Gkazas (Belgium and the Netherlands), Zeta (Sweden), Carapelli and Tesco Finest publish no number for any of their oils.

The exception is in the UK. Odysea's "Good For You" Polyphenol extra virgin olive oil, sold at Waitrose and Sainsbury's in a 250ml tin, guarantees at least 250 mg/kg until its best-before date. That is the EU health claim level, at the top of the supermarket range and below every oil on this list.

## How to choose a high-polyphenol olive oil

- **A lab report you can open.** A number on a label means more when the certificate behind it is published, with the lab and the method named.
- **A recent harvest date.** Polyphenols fall from the day the oil is pressed: about 42% in the first year, even when stored well. An oil tested at 800 mg/kg holds about 464 a year later.
- **Dark glass or a tin.** Olive oil has three enemies: light, oxygen and heat.

More on reading a certificate in [How To Read An Olive Oil Lab Report](/blog/how-to-read-olive-oil-lab-analysis).

## FAQ

**Which olive oil has the highest polyphenols you can buy in Europe?**

Laconiko ZOI, with 1,196 mg/kg oleocanthal + oleacein in its 2025/26 analysis and 1,440 in its 2026/27 analysis. It ships to Europe from the US by express courier. The highest oils with a European shop or retailer are The Governor Limited (875) and ATTIMO Coratina (807).

**Where can I buy high-polyphenol olive oil in the UK?**

The Governor sells from its own UK shop, Healf stocks Olivea and Blueprint, and OLV Limits, PJ Kabos, Morocco Gold and ATTIMO ship to the UK. In supermarkets, Odysea's polyphenol oil at Waitrose and Sainsbury's guarantees at least 250 mg/kg.

**Why is high-polyphenol olive oil more expensive?**

Olives picked early, while still green, give less oil and more polyphenols. The oils on this list cost €16–80 per 500ml.

**Do polyphenol numbers change every harvest?**

Yes. Each harvest is a different oil: Laconiko ZOI went from 1,397 mg/kg in 2025/26 to 1,552 in 2026/27. A figure only describes the harvest it was measured on.

**Why is my favourite oil missing?**

Either its producer publishes no polyphenol figure, or it can't be bought from Europe, or we missed it. Producers can submit an analysis on the [Olive Oil Polyphenol Index](/polyphenol-index) page.
